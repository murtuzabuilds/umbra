The first wave of startups got bought

Between 2024 and 2025 the leading AI security startups were bought by platform vendors: Robust Intelligence by Cisco, Protect AI by Palo Alto Networks, Lakera by Check Point, Prompt Security by SentinelOne. That tells me the problem is real. It also means most of these tools now sit inside large security suites, priced for big enterprises and built mainly to block things.

The regulation is arriving in stages

The EU AI Act's transparency duties (Article 50) apply from 2 August 2026. The 2026 Digital Omnibus moved the stand-alone high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027. They were delayed, not dropped, and companies will be asked to show evidence of how they govern AI.

02  What I believe

For the last few years most of the energy has gone into building with AI. Very little has gone into keeping track of it. I think the harder problem now is governing the AI that is already inside every company, and doing it without slowing people down.

IDEA 1

Unapproved AI use tells you what people need.

When someone uses an unapproved tool, it is usually because they have a job to do and nothing approved helps them do it. Umbra treats that as useful information. It groups the activity by team and task and gives the CIO a ranked list of where an approved tool would help most. This is where my digital transformation work comes in.

IDEA 2

Let AI help write the rules, but not enforce them.

Umbra's AI layer, Corona, drafts rules from plain English, spots unregistered AI services and explains incidents. The decision to block or allow is always made by fixed rules that give the same answer every time. Anything Corona writes is tested against past traffic and approved by a person before it goes live.

IDEA 3

Treat AI agents like employees with access.

Agents now call other agents that call tools, sometimes three steps deep, and often nobody owns the chain. Umbra gives each agent a named owner and a written list of what it is allowed to do, checks every call against it, and estimates how much data someone could reach if the agent were compromised.

04  Principles

Six decisions I made early and kept.

01

See first, then govern

A company cannot manage AI use it cannot see, so discovery comes first. It works from logs the company already has, with nothing to install.

02

Predictable enforcement

Blocking and masking are done by fixed rules and tested detectors. The same input always gets the same decision, with a record an auditor can follow. No AI model makes that call.

03

Guide people before blocking them

Where it is safe, Umbra masks the sensitive part and lets the request through, or suggests an approved tool. Hard blocks are kept for passwords, keys and regulated data.

04

Every agent has an owner

No agent runs without a named person responsible for it and a clear list of what it may do. More freedom is given deliberately, as trust builds.

05

Evidence as a side effect

Every decision Umbra makes is already logged, so audit evidence can be exported at any time instead of pulled together by hand before an audit.

06

Measure adoption

The main metric is the share of AI work done in approved tools. It only goes up when people are both protected and well served, which is the outcome both security and the business want.

05  The product

A console for the whole picture.

Everything below works in the live console, which runs on a tested engine (16 tests). The demo company is Kestrel Mutual, a made-up insurer with 2,400 staff and 30 days of generated activity.

Totality

How much of your AI use you can actually see

Totality is the share of AI activity Umbra can see and classify. Next to it are the share of work on approved tools, how much sensitive data was stopped, how many incidents are open, and a short weekly summary from Corona with the two most useful next steps.

Constellation

Every AI service on one map

Distance from the centre shows risk, size shows usage, and colour shows whether it is approved. Risk is computed from facts a buyer can verify: whether the vendor trains on inputs, how long it keeps them, which attestations it holds and where it processes data. Corona can also recognise AI services nobody registered, just from the shape of the traffic.

Inspect

Each prompt checked, with the reasons shown

The detectors check that a match is real before flagging it: card numbers must pass the Luhn check, bank account numbers the IBAN checksum, and US social security numbers the issuing rules. Each decision shows what was actually sent and which rules applied.

  • Secrets, health data, personal and financial data, source code, codenames, confidentiality markers
  • Stable placeholders, so a redacted prompt still makes sense to the model
Agents

Agents with owners and limits

The graph shows which people use which agents, and which agents call other agents and tools. Calls outside an agent's allowed list show in dashed red. It also shows indirect access: in the demo, the broker assistant can reach financial data it was never given, because it is allowed to call an agent that has it.

  • Shadow agent detection, quarantine and registration
  • Prompt injection and exfiltration scanning for agent payloads
Corona and the policy replay

Write a rule in plain English, then test it on last month

Corona turns a sentence into a rule, points out anything it was unsure about, and restates the rule in English so a reviewer can check it. Before the rule goes live, Umbra replays the last 30 days against it and shows how many decisions would change, and for which people and teams.

Incidents

Fewer alerts, clearer decisions

Umbra groups related events into one case per decision someone has to make. Cases the rules already handled are closed automatically, with the evidence kept. Corona writes a short summary for the analyst and a friendlier note for the employee involved.

Demand Map

Where your company wants to use AI

Unapproved usage is grouped by team and task, then scored by how often it happens, how much of it runs on unapproved tools, and whether it is growing. The result is a now, next and later list of where to roll out approved AI. This is the part that turns a security tool into a planning tool.

Assurance

Audit evidence on demand

Eight controls are measured from what the product is actually doing and mapped to the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI RMF and SOC 2. You can export an evidence pack, or a full inventory of AI models, services and agents in the CycloneDX format. A five-level maturity scale gives the board a simple way to track progress.

Employee view

What employees actually see

Sensitive details are masked before sending, there is a one-click switch to the approved tool, and when nothing is wrong Umbra stays out of the way. The check runs on the person's own device before anything is sent.

08  Go to market

Start with mid-sized, regulated companies.

Ideal customer

Regulated companies of 500 to 5,000 people (insurance, fintech, health, legal) with a lean security team, an AI mandate from the board, and no time for a six-month rollout. The CISO would buy it, and the CIO would back it, because the Demand Map is useful to them.

First step: a free 48-hour scan

The company connects its sign-in provider and web proxy logs, read-only, with nothing to install. Two days later it gets a map of its AI use and a Demand Map. That one report shows both the risk and the opportunity, and it is easy for a CISO to share with the board.

Channels

Managed security providers who need to cover AI for their clients, cyber insurers who are starting to ask about AI controls, and audit firms that need evidence they can test.

How it grows

The free scan brings companies in, a sales team helps them move to paid plans, and per-agent pricing grows as they run more agents. Revenue grows as the customer's own AI use grows.

Compared with network security tools

They see traffic and allow or block it. Umbra adds prompt-level guidance, agent controls and the Demand Map, and can use their logs instead of replacing them.

Compared with AI firewalls

09  Roadmap and risks

What comes next, and what could go wrong.

Now

  • Discovery from logs and app grants
  • Deterministic inspection and redaction
  • Policies with traces, triage, coaching
  • Evidence packs

Next

  • Corona running on a real AI model, limited to producing valid rules
  • The agent gateway ready for production
  • Tool approval workflow with vendor facts pre-filled
  • AI bill of materials in customer pipelines

Later

  • An open format for agent permissions that works across platforms
  • Reports that cyber insurers can accept
  • Automatic fixes, always approved by a person
RiskHow I would handle it
Too many false alarmsGuidance is the default, hard blocks are kept for secrets and regulated data, and every rule change is tested on past traffic first. The override rate is tracked closely.
Employees feel watchedChecks run on the employee's device, and Umbra stores what it found rather than the text itself. People can see exactly what was masked and why, which also makes it easier to explain to works councils.
Big security suites add basic AI featuresFocus on what they do least well: fast setup for mid-sized companies, agent controls, and the Demand Map, which gives the CIO a reason to buy.
The AI layer gets fooledCorona never makes the final decision. What it produces is turned into fixed rules, tested and approved by a person first.
10  Brand

The mark is an eclipse.

The umbra is the darkest part of a shadow. The mark is a dark disc, standing for the AI a company cannot see, with a ring of particles around it, standing for what Umbra shows. The particles thin out away from the edge like a real eclipse, and a few are violet to suggest the signals Corona picks up. I built it from the same square particles as my personal logo so the two feel related. In motion the corona turns, twinkles and leans toward your cursor, and a small flare circles the rim.
