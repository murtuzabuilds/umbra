Umbra v1: govern every AI a company uses.

The product-level PRD: who Umbra is for, what v1 must prove, the ten capabilities in scope, where AI is and is not used, and how a pilot is judged.

ProductUmbraTypeProduct PRD
AuthorMurtuza, product managerVersion1.0, 16 November 2026
StatusApproved scope, v1 built in demoFeature PRDsTool approval · Governed agents

This is an example PRD written for the case study. Umbra has no customers yet, Kestrel Mutual is a made-up insurer, and reviewers are roles, not people.

01  Summary

Govern every AI a company uses, without becoming the reason it falls behind.

Staff at regulated companies already use AI tools nobody approved, and they paste sensitive data into them. Security can neither see this nor stop it without blocking the work people need done.

Umbra v1 gives a security lead at a company of 500 to 5,000 people one place to see every AI tool and agent in use, stop sensitive data before it leaves, turn written policy into enforced rules, and show an auditor the evidence. It treats unapproved use as a signal of what people need, and turns it into a plan for which approved AI to fund.

One rule runs through the whole product: AI suggests, fixed rules decide, and a person approves.

The risk is real, and the tools are built for someone else.

SignalWhat it tells us

63% of breached organizations had no AI governance policy, or were still writing one (same report)Most companies are governing AI on paper, if at all.

04  Goals and non-goals

What v1 must prove, and what it leaves alone.

Goals

  1. Show a company the AI it actually uses within 48 hours, with nothing installed.
  2. Stop regulated data and secrets from reaching unapproved AI, while letting ordinary work through.
  3. Turn a written policy into an enforced rule in minutes, with a person approving every change.
  4. Produce audit evidence as a side effect of running, not as a project before an audit.
  5. Move AI work onto approved tools, measured every week.

Non-goals

  1. Securing AI models a company builds itself. Governance platforms and runtime security tools do that.
  2. Running agents day to day. That is Outpost's job.
  3. Replacing the network security suite. Umbra reads its logs and sits beside it.
  4. Letting any model make an enforcement decision, now or later.
05  Product principles

Six rules that settle most arguments early.

PrincipleWhat it means in practice
See first, then governDiscovery ships before enforcement, from logs the company already has.
Predictable enforcementBlocking and masking are fixed rules and tested detectors. The same input always gets the same decision.
Guide before blockingMask or coach by default. Hard blocks are kept for passwords, keys and regulated data.
Every agent has an ownerNo agent runs without a named person and a written list of what it may do.
Evidence as a side effectEvery decision is logged in a form an auditor can follow.
Measure adoptionThe main metric rises only when people are both protected and well served.

07  Where AI is used

AI suggests. Fixed rules decide.

JobWho does itWhy
Draft a rule from a sentenceModel (gpt-oss-120b on Groq), checked against the rule languagePeople write policy the way they talk. On 51 test sentences: 46 right against 28 for rules alone, 0 unsafe.
Summarize an incidentModel, with a check that rejects any number not in the recordSaves the analyst time. A wrong number never reaches the screen.
Allow, mask or block a promptFixed rules and verified detectorsMust give the same answer every time, with a trace an auditor can follow.
Spot hidden instructions and unknown endpointsFixed rulesA model reading a hostile message can be talked round by it.
Switch a rule onA personEvery change to enforcement has a name against it.
08  Non-functional requirements

The promises a security buyer will test.

AreaRequirement
PrivacyChecks run on the employee's device. Umbra stores what it found, not the prompt text.
DeterminismThe same input always gets the same enforcement decision.
Fail closedIf the model is down for 8 seconds, drafting falls back to the rules-based parser and says so. Enforcement never depends on the model.
SpeedA drafted rule in under one second at the median (481 ms measured).
DeploymentThe first scan installs nothing and reads logs read-only, so it can pass a security review first.
CostThe model is called when someone writes a policy, not on every prompt, so serving cost does not grow with staff AI use.

10  Release plan, risks and open questions

Release in the order a buyer can say yes.

Release 1

Scan

48-hour discovery, the AI map, the Demand Map and a maturity baseline. Free.

Release 2

Govern

Inspection, masking, coaching, policies with Corona and replay, incidents.

Release 3

Assure

Continuous control evidence, framework mappings and the AI bill of materials.

Release 4

Agents

Owners, permissions and approved models for agents, handed to Outpost.

RiskResponse
Too many false alarmsGuidance by default, every rule replayed on past traffic first, override rate tracked as a guardrail.
Employees feel watchedChecks on the device, findings stored instead of text, and people can see what was masked and why.
Large suites add basic AI featuresCompete where they are weakest: fast setup for mid-sized companies, agent owners and the Demand Map.
The AI layer gets fooledCorona never decides. Its drafts are checked, replayed and approved by a person.

Open questions

  1. Can security leads already see most AI use with the tools they have? If yes, discovery is not the wedge.
  2. Will a CIO fund anything from the Demand Map? If not, Umbra is a security purchase only.
  3. Does drafting save analysts real time against an answer key? The five-analyst test is not yet run.
  4. Who has the budget: security, risk or the AI program?
